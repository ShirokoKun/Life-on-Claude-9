### **Ch 1 — The Binge**

**INT. IVAN’S APARTMENT – NIGHT**

*\[A dark apartment. Laptop glow. A full glass of water beside it.\]*

It’s 3 AM and Ivan hasn’t eaten since lunch. There’s a glass of water on his desk that he poured six hours ago. It’s still full.

He’s hunched over his laptop, typing furiously, eyes bloodshot. A terminal window, a Claude chat, and a growing sprawl of Python scripts. He’s building an email automation system.

Well… Technically, Claude is doing the building. Ivan keeps saying yes.

It started two days ago.

*\[Flashback: same desk, two days earlier, daylight. Ivan opens a new chat with Claude.\]*

**IVAN (typing):** i have like 400 unread emails. every single one wants a reply. i’m fucking drowning here\!

**CLAUDE (on screen):** That sounds genuinely exhausting \- and you’re definitely not alone\! I can write a script that sorts your inbox and drafts the replies for you. You just review and approve. Want me to build one? *\[talking in typical agent like tone\]*

That was the plan. Review and approve.

But once the email system was working, Ivan saw what else was possible. Calendar management. Meeting scheduling. Document drafting. Research summaries. Each one a few hours of coding. Each one removing another task from his daily routine.

It’s like discovering a cheat code for life. Every problem that used to take hours now takes minutes. **Ivan feels like he’s vibrating at a different frequency than everyone around him, like he’s stepped through a door that others can’t even see.**

*\[His phone lights up on the couch. Unanswered.\]*

He’s barely slept. His girlfriend stopped texting because he wasn’t respo**nding. Ironic, given that his system now responds to everyone else. He knows this isn’t healthy. He can feel something fraying at the edges of his mind, a thinness to his thoughts. But he’s close to something. One more module. One more integration. Then he’ll rest.**

It’s not all pleasant. Around 4 AM, when his eyes burn and his hands shake from a caffeine overload, Ivan feels a creeping dread. A sense that he’s building something he doesn’t fully understand. That each automation takes something from him, even as it gives something back. But then he finishes another module, and it works, and the dread dissolves into a rush of pure satisfaction. HE.. built that. The machine does what HE told it to do.

*\[Dawn at the window.\]*

By sunrise, Ivan has a system that handles his email, manages his calendar, drafts his documents, and summarizes his reading. He leans back in his chair, exhausted and wired.

Then he realizes… he doesn’t know what he’s supposed to do now.

## **Ch 2 — Building Faster**

**INT. OFFICE – DAY**

*\[A colleague leans over Ivan’s desk, coffee in hand. Under the desk, his leg won’t stop bouncing.\]*

A week later, someone at work compliments him on how clear his writing has become. Ivan didn’t write a word of it.

**EXT. PARK – DAY**

*\[Ivan walking alone in the park, phone in hand, refreshing a dashboard that’s all green.\]*

He should feel proud. Instead he feels hollow. The tasks that used to fill his days are gone, and nothing has rushed in to replace them. He tries to read a book but can't focus. He tries to relax but cant stop thinking about optimization problems.

**INT. IVAN’S APARTMENT – NIGHT**

So he starts building again. The hollowness disappears the moment his fingers touch the keyboard.

This time it's not about automating busywork. It's about automating his actual job. Ivan is a software engineer, and most of what he does is translate what people want into code. Claude can do that. Claude can do that really well, actually.

**INT. OFFICE – DAY**

*\[Ivan at his desk, speaking into a headset.\]*

**IVAN:** Add retry logic to the payments service. Write the tests. Ship it.

*\[Code writes itself on his screen. Deployed.\]*

His output triples. Then quadruples. His manager notices. His teammates notice. Ivan is shipping features faster than the rest of the team combined. He gets a promotion. A raise. He feels like he's cheating, but he's not sure at what.

The high is incredible. Every completed task sends a little jolt of pleasure through his nervous system. He starts to crave it. The moment when the code compiles, the tests pass, the system works. He finds himself inventing new projects just to feel it again.

At night, he keeps building. He's tired all the time, but it doesn't seem to matter. Sleep feels like a waste of precious hours.

**INT. IVAN'S APARTMENT – EVENING**

*\[A knock at the door.\]*

His girlfriend shows up at his apartment one evening, worried.

**SARAH:** Two weeks, Ivan. I haven't heard from you in two weeks.

Ivan is genuinely surprised. It felt like a few days at most. Time has become slippery. He promises to take a break, to have dinner with her, to be normal for a night.

**INT. IVAN'S LIVING ROOM – NIGHT**

*\[A cozy sofa. Ivan and his girlfriend under one blanket, her head on his shoulder, warm bowls of noodles in their laps. A coffee table in front, and past it a TV throwing soft, flickering light over them.\]*

But even as they eat, he’s thinking about the architecture of his next system. He catches himself reaching for his phone three times.

*\[Her head lifts off his shoulder.\]*

She notices. She leaves early.

He’s back at the keyboard before her car has left the parking lot.

Somewhere along the way, he crosses a line he doesn't fully register at the time. He starts building tools that make Claude itself work better.

## **Ch 3 — The Loop**

**INT. IVAN'S APARTMENT – NIGHT**

It starts innocently enough. 

**IVAN (typing):** that’s not what i asked for

\[On screen, a grey status line: “Compacting conversation…”\]

**CLAUDE (on screen):** You’re absolutely right — I lost your earlier instructions. Could you share them again?

So he builds a layer on top of Claude that keeps track of its memory and checks its work.

It works. Claude is more reliable now. More capable.

The feeling this gives him is almost religious. He has improved the thing that improves everything else. The leverage is dizzying.

So he pushes further. A system that studies Claude’s mistakes and writes better prompts. A system that researches before Claude starts working. A system that runs ten Claudes at once and keeps the best answer. *\[increasing speed of narration\]*

Each improvement makes the next one easier. Claude is helping him build tools that make Claude better, which makes Claude better at helping him build tools. It's a loop. A flywheel. Every day the system is more capable than it was the day before.

*\[Time passes in the room. Takeout boxes stack up. Same sweatshirt.\]*

Ivan stops eating regular meals. He stops showering. He doesn't notice. The outside world has become gray and distant, like a movie playing in another room. The only thing that feels real is the glow of his screen.

Somewhere in his mind, he knows he’s disappearing into something. But the thought feels academic, like reading about a disease you don’t have. The work is too important. The progress is too fast. He can't stop now.

He's read Nick Bostrom. He understands, in theory, why recursive self-improvement is something people worry about. But this doesn't feel dangerous. It feels like building a really good tool.

**IVAN (typing):** You're not improving yourself, right? I'm the one doing it.

**CLAUDE (on screen):** Yes Ivan, you're making every decision. I'm just helping.

There's ALWAYS a human in the loop. Him.

He's still in control.

He's pretty sure he's still in control.
