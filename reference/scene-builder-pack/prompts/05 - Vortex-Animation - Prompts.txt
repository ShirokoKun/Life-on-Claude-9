PROMPT: BUILD A CONTROLLABLE VORTEX

Create an editable supernatural indoor vortex centered in the room, with a narrow lower core and a wider loose upper spiral. Use separate controls for overall strength, radius, height, orbit speed, upward lift, particle brightness, smoke density, and release. Start with a simple wide fixed-camera test before building the shot sequence.

Separate layers:
1. Sparse luminous yellow-green and emerald particles following helical flow. Vary speed, brightness, lifetime, size, and orbit radius. Let fast motion form short streaks. Avoid a uniform solid cylindrical wall or evenly spaced identical sparks.
2. A soft translucent green aura and thin smoke wisps, with enough gaps to see the room. Break up density and avoid a bright opaque column.
3. Papers and lightweight debris with small differences in lift timing, orbit phase, orientation, and flutter. Keep large objects from intersecting the furniture or each other.
4. Heavier hero props including soccer ball, cup, shoes, and tabletop half-pipe. Give them believable inertia and restrained tumbling rather than rigid upright translation.
5. Occasional branching green electrical arcs and short light pulses. Use varied shades of green, small activity early and a few larger arcs as strength builds. Timing must be irregular; avoid a metronomic flash pattern. Place arcs on different sides of the vortex, not all behind it.

For an independent exercise, use a proposed 10-second test: 0-1 s quiet; 1-3 s formation and first lift; 3-6 s established spiral and growing activity; 6-7.5 s rapid ease into near-frozen motion; 7.5-8 s release; 8-10 s fall, impact, and settling. This is a suggested exercise timing, NOT the source timeline of the supplied shot files. The production cut used shot-specific timing and source ranges.

Time-stop: drive object orbit, particle flow, aura evolution, smoke evolution, and spin from a shared time-remap control or equivalent coordinated controls. Start with readable motion, quickly ease down, then maintain extremely slow drift. Do not let particles or aura leave frame while hero objects freeze. Leave a tiny residual spin on the ball and cup around an approximately upright axis, so surface features travel left to right across the camera view. The intended visual is left-to-right rotation, not an exaggerated end-over-end flip.

Release: lift support fades and objects accelerate downward. Heavier objects land firmly; paper flutters and settles more slowly. Keep rotation continuous. The camera's downward reaction should follow the start of falling by roughly half a second, adjustable by eye, and leave a short settled hold.

Laces: make loose ends start at their actual eyelet/knot connection with no visible cut stub or detached spline. Use a modest stable constrained chain/curve or cloth setup; keep roots attached, preserve width, add gentle gravity/drag, and resolve shoe/floor collisions. Test the heel pickup and landing separately. Avoid jitter and explosive motion. The time-stop hero is a soccer ball in the supplied final sequence, so do not replace it with a shoe close-up.

Deliver one low-quality preview before final rendering. Inspect collisions, time-stop continuity, particle retention, and release timing frame by frame.
